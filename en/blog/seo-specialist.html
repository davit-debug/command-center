What Does an SEO Specialist Do? A Complete Guide

October 16, 2026

Almost no one clicks to the second page of search results. This single habit has given rise to an entire profession. Some consider these professionals marketers, others see them as programmers, but in reality, the field combines elements of both.

A website can have the best product, competitive pricing, and outstanding service, but if Google leaves it beyond the first page, customers simply won't find it. This invisibility translates to real losses for a business: a person actively searching for a product ends up with a competitor because they appeared on the first page. Tackling this challenge is the job of an SEO specialist. Their main task is to ensure that search engines fully understand and trust a website, showing it to the target audience at the right time and place. Results come after months of systematic work, analysis, and continuous optimization.

What Does an SEO Specialist's Daily Workflow Involve?

An SEO specialist's workday typically begins with checking data and evaluating current results. They monitor how a website's positions are changing in search engines, which pages are seeing an increase or decrease in organic visits, what keywords the site is ranking for, and how well these results align with set goals. Key sources of information at this stage are Google Search Console, Google Analytics, and other SEO-related tools. The data gathered helps the specialist identify problems, determine which pages need improvement, and see which strategies are working effectively.

The next part of the job involves direct site optimization. An SEO specialist researches keywords and user search intent, analyzes competitors' pages, and checks if the website itself is properly structured. Their work might include improving page titles and descriptions, changing content structure, adding internal links, identifying technical issues, and checking page load speed and mobile performance. If new content is needed, the specialist also defines the topics and search queries worth targeting.

Furthermore, they constantly monitor the results of implemented changes and adjust the strategy as needed. This process includes discovering new search queries, keeping up with search engine updates, and documenting the work in reports. Consequently, an SEO specialist's daily routine brings together a wide range of tasks: research, technical analysis, content work, data evaluation, and planning.

The Four Pillars of SEO: Technical, On-Page, Off-Page, and AI SEO

To ensure search engines can fully understand a website and rank it in top positions, this complex, multi-faceted process is divided into several key areas.

The first and most important is technical SEO, which forms the foundation of a website. It answers the main question: can Google discover, read, and add the site's pages to its index? This area includes site structure, robots.txt and sitemap files, page load speed, mobile performance, duplicate content management, and more. A specialist working on technical SEO often collaborates with programmers. The results of their work are almost invisible to the user, but if this foundation isn't solid, other SEO activities won't deliver the desired results.

The second area is On-page SEO, which covers everything we control within the page itself. At this stage, the SEO specialist works on page titles, meta descriptions, H1-H3 structure, text quality, image optimization, and internal links. A well-optimized page must cater to two parties simultaneously: the user, who needs a useful and clear answer, and the search engine, which needs to understand what the page is about and how well it matches a specific query.

The third area is Off-page SEO, which involves activities that take place outside the website. One of its main components is link building, as a link from another credible website acts as a recommendation for search engines. Therefore, an SEO specialist constantly seeks out and evaluates the site's backlink profile, aiming to grow the brand's authority organically.

The fourth area is AI SEO, which is becoming increasingly relevant as search engines evolve. Today, users often ask questions in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity and get ready-made answers right there. Consequently, an SEO specialist now has to consider how AI systems perceive and use information about a specific brand. This requires reliable sources, clearly formulated information, good structure, and content whose meaning and context can be easily recognized by AI systems.

Why Does Your Business Need an SEO Specialist?

After analyzing all four areas, a natural question arises: what does a business get out of all this? The fact is, a well-optimized page can attract users for months and even years, while every new article, category page, or well-planned internal link further strengthens the site's organic visibility.

As a result, the more high-quality pages a site accumulates and the more firmly it holds its positions for relevant search queries, the more opportunities arise to attract new customers. It's especially important that people who arrive via search have a specific interest—they are already looking for a product, service, or an answer to a question. Such a visitor often has a clearer need, which increases the chances of them taking the desired action.

The work of an SEO specialist is dedicated to accumulating and managing these results. They monitor which pages are performing well, where potential customers are being lost, and which queries are creating new opportunities. This is why monthly SEO services are particularly important for businesses that aim to achieve stable, long-term results from organic search. Based on the data obtained, the site's development process can be planned more strategically.

Simply put, an SEO specialist provides a business with information that is often difficult to obtain through other channels: what the target audience is searching for, what questions they have before making a purchase, and where new demand is emerging. In this sense, keyword research is a kind of demand map. It can reveal which products are gaining interest, in which regions a specific service is being sought, and where the site is lagging behind competitors. Such data directly helps a business with product development, assortment selection, and improving communication with clients.

What Skills Should a Professional SEO Specialist Have?

Only a specialist with a combination of technical and analytical skills can achieve results that align with business goals. First and foremost, analytical thinking is required. An SEO specialist must be able to read data and see the cause behind it. A drop in traffic on a graph means nothing if you can't determine whether it's a technical error, a competitor's move, or a search engine update. This is complemented by technical knowledge. You don't have to be a programmer, but understanding the basic principles of HTML, site structure, indexing, and page loading is necessary to correctly identify problems and clearly formulate tasks for a developer.

Another crucial skill is understanding search intent. An SEO specialist must determine what a person is really looking for when they type a specific keyword and what kind of answer will satisfy their query.

Along with technical knowledge, an SEO specialist needs communication and content skills. They must be able to create clear briefs for copywriters and justify necessary changes. Interacting with the team is equally important.

Patience is also essential in this profession, as the results of implemented changes often become noticeable only after several weeks or months. Therefore, a specialist must set realistic expectations and clearly explain what results are possible and in what timeframe.

Finally, one of the most important skills in SEO is a desire for continuous learning. Search engine algorithms, user behavior, and AI platforms are changing rapidly, so a professional SEO specialist systematically tests new approaches, measures results, and adapts their strategy when necessary. Learning from mistakes is part of the job; the key is for the specialist to realize in time what needs to be done differently next time.

How to Become an SEO Specialist: First Steps in the Profession

If you want to learn SEO but don't know where to start or which resources and practical methods to use, you can check out our guide: "How to Learn SEO," where we discuss the different stages of this process in detail from start to finish.

In general, the first step is to understand what SEO is, how Google finds and processes website pages, what search intent means, and what factors affect a site's visibility. After that, you can delve deeper into individual areas—Technical SEO, On-page, Off-page, and AI SEO.

Of course, it's difficult to master this profession with theoretical knowledge alone. Therefore, alongside learning, it's essential to observe real websites and test various changes. At this stage, using tools like Google Search Console, Google Analytics, Ahrefs, and Screaming Frog is particularly important.

The next step is to gain practical experience. Work on your own project. You don't need a large budget for this. A small blog, a personal site, or a friend's business website where you can freely make changes and observe the results is enough. Choose a few search queries, improve the corresponding pages, add internal links, and watch what changes over the following weeks and months. Such a project will also give you the opportunity to create your first portfolio, as you'll be able to show an employer or client not just what you know, but what you've actually done and the results you've achieved. If you prefer structured learning, a professional course can be a great starting point—for example, the 10xSEO course, where you can work on practical tasks alongside theoretical knowledge.

In conclusion, as mentioned above, SEO is a field where new tools constantly emerge, search engine approaches change, and the influence of AI on search grows. Therefore, professional development is a daily part of this job. The more sites you work on, the better you'll become at identifying different problems, evaluating results, and making the right decisions. In this process, applying knowledge in practice, measuring outcomes, and understanding why a particular decision worked or didn't work is crucial.
